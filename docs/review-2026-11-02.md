# Security review of 2 November 2026

An AI review, not an audit. Codex (gpt-6-astra, high reasoning) read the repository at commit
`e1bc35b` read-only, after the hackathon submission, and reported what a security judge would find
in an hour. Each finding was verified by hand against the source before anything was changed. The
fixes are in the commits named below; the sandbox attack suite, the contract's unit tests and the
three Node packages' tests pass after them.

## Findings

| # | Severity | Where | Finding | Verified | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical (would have applied to any public deployment; none existed) | `tests/security/examples/testnet.rs`, sub-account creation | The testnet bootstrap created `intents.`, `agent.`, `shop.` and `attacker.` without choosing keys. near-workspaces 0.23 then installs a full-access key derived from the public seed string `"subaccount.seed"` (`operations.rs:459` of the crate). Anyone could have taken those accounts, including our Intents copy, after `make testnet`. | Yes: the crate's `transact()` falls back to `SecretKey::from_seed(ED25519, "subaccount.seed")`. The bootstrap had not been run on testnet, so no account was exposed. | Each sub-account is created with a fresh random key; after setup the bootstrap deletes every full-access key it used, so `vault.`, `intents.`, `shop.` and `attacker.` end with no key and `agent.` with only its `request_payment` keys. It then reads the key inventory on chain and refuses to write `target/testnet.json` unless that holds. |
| 2 | Significant (demo components) | `demo/merchant/src/server.ts`, `/deliver` | The merchant marked the invoice paid before returning the report. A lost response left the buyer unable to collect: the retry answered "already paid". | Yes. | `InvoiceStore.redeliver`: the holder of the delivery token may collect a paid invoice again, only with the same token and the same payment transaction. An observer of the payment still gets nothing. Test added in `demo/merchant/test`. |
| 3 | Significant (demo components) | `app/agent/src/agent.ts`, purchases awaiting approval | Purchases waiting for the owner live only in memory. A runner restart before the approval loses the delivery token, so that report is never collected; the payment itself is unaffected. | Yes. | Documented as a limitation in the README. Not fixed: persisting the tokens is straightforward but was not part of the demo's scope, and the merchant's redelivery (finding 2) is the half that makes a persisted runner recoverable. |
| 4 | Significant (owner app) | `app/web/src/chain.ts`, token metadata | A metadata read failure was replaced by `{ decimals: 0 }`, which mis-scales every amount and makes the demo strip compute a negative BigInt exponent, crashing the page. | Yes. | The failure propagates; the app shows the error instead of wrong numbers. |
| 5 | Minor (agent runner) | `app/agent/src/server.ts`, `POST /buy` | The `compromised` flag was honoured even with `DEMO_ATTACKS` off. | Yes. | Refused with 403 unless demo attacks are enabled. |

Codex found no way for the agent to exceed the contract's spending limits. The known limitations it
restated (owner-asserted recovery of a stuck payment, repeated payment of the same invoice as a new
payment within limits, the UTC-midnight budget boundary) are in the threat model and the README.

## Documentation corrections from the same review

- `docs/acceptance.md`: the mutation check covers five targeted mutations, not "each test"; T1 and
  T2 share one contract-level test, with the agent-level T1 in the e2e script; T6 is proved by
  receipt ordering, not a mid-flight `budget()` read; `t16a` accepts either race winner; `t17d`
  measures one configuration. The October demo plan, day-2 gate and mainnet evidence list are
  marked as the plan, with the actual state in the README.
- `docs/architecture.md`: the app has pause and unpause, not the policy and merchant modals; the
  code-hash and key-list checks are the bootstrap's, not the app's; the demo strip has seven steps.
- README: the setup checklist dates from 25 October, not 1 November; the repository went public and
  was submitted on 2 November; the two possible outcomes of the eight-at-once step are explained
  (five settle and three `OverDaily` after the approval; seven settle and one `TooManyPending` while
  a payment still waits, which is the run the landing page shows).
- `make check-live` rerun on 2 November: both pinned binaries equal the live mainnet code at block
  222693527 (`evidence/local-run.md`).
- A LICENSE (MIT) and a CI workflow for the unit-level checks were missing; added.

## What the review recommended that is still open

In its order: the testnet run with a real wallet and the T20 screenshot; a purchase driven by a real
model on NEAR AI Cloud; a longer video showing both; a hosted testnet demo through judging; a tiny
mainnet pilot against the real `intents.near`; a second merchant with price and quality choice to
give the Agent Economy angle substance; benchmarks of the approval, failure and maximum-active
paths. The README's status section records which of these are done.
